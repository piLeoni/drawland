# Screens: bitmaps and text

## Purpose

The same graph, painted on a grid instead of drawn on paper: a 1-bit **bitmap** (every pixel black or white; PNG or PBM) or **text** cells (a terminal; plain text, ANSI truecolour, HTML, or a PNG picture of the cells). `output({ screen })` makes the output a screen, and `paint(...)` entries cover it, bottom first. A plotter's `draw` entries and `compose` (masks, optimize) are not used on a screen: a layer's `halo` is how it clears what lies under it.

## Units

On a screen the output's unit is the screen's own, not the millimetre: a **pixel** on a bitmap, a **character** on text. Every length a profile writes (a buffer, an offset, `page.width`, a shape's points) counts them, and `scale` is the ground one of them spans: `scale: "3m"` is three metres a pixel (or a character). Inside, the scale stays what it is on paper, the ground a unit spans in mm (`"1:10000"` is a millimetre spanning 10 m, `"3m"` a unit spanning 3 m, both `Scale(10000.0)`/`Scale(3000.0)`), so `Output`, `Frame` and every op read it unchanged; `world.scale` is that number. A character is one unit across and two down (a row is as tall as two characters are wide), so geometry stays square and a circle stays round on the screen; a dot (`CELL_DOTS`, 2 × 4 a character, a Braille character's grid) is half a unit. `fit_screen` writes the size (`width` pixels or columns, `height` pixels or twice the rows) into `width` and `height`; `output()` refuses `width`, `height`, `padding` and `slices` beside a `screen`. A text screen is painted on its dot grid (`2w × 4h`) where it needs sub-cell detail (Braille, quarter blocks) and on its cells otherwise. A length written with a unit (`"2mm"`) still reads as that many mm and so as that many units; write bare numbers on a screen.

## Styling by zoom and feature

A look's property can be a **Mapbox GL expression** (`expr.rs`), in Mapbox's own syntax, so a style written for a web map reads the same here: `["step", ["zoom"], "spleen-6x12", 9, "spleen-8x16"]`, `["match", ["get", "class"], "city", 5, null]`, `["interpolate", ["linear"], ["zoom"], 12, 1, 18, 4]`. A paint entry also takes Mapbox's `minzoom`, `maxzoom` and `filter`. An array led by an operator's name is an expression, any other value itself (`["literal", …]` for an array that would read as one). The operators are a subset of Mapbox's (`OPERATORS`: `get has zoom coalesce match case step interpolate`, comparisons, `all any !`, arithmetic, conversions, `concat`), plus `["scale"]`, the ground a unit spans in metres; a Mapbox operator not understood is refused by name, never read as a plain array. Adding one is an arm in `Expr::parse`, an arm in `Expr::eval` and its name in `OPERATORS`; an expression can stand wherever a value is read per frame, so a paper look can take them the same way when it wants them.

- **The zoom** is Mapbox's at the centre: the web Mercator world 512 units across at zoom 0, a unit being a pixel on a bitmap, a character on text (so a text style's zooms sit some three below a pixel style's, a character being about eight pixels), a millimetre on paper (`Frame::new(metres a unit, lat)`; `View::frame` for a moving screen).
- **When it is evaluated**: at each frame, not when the profile runs. A moving screen paints many zooms from one run of the graph (a scene), so a value the script picked from `world.scale` would be wrong one step later. `grammar_run::paint` hands back `Layer`s, the looks as written (`Style`); `resolve(layers, frame)` gives a frame's `Painted` layers (`Scene::render_recalling` per view; `Drawer::paint_recalling` and `paint_globe` at the output's own frame). Layers outside their zooms are left out.
- **Per feature**: only names keep their features' tags (`Layer::tags`, by `Named::feature`, kept only when the look or the filter reads them); an area or lines are geometry made from features (unions, differences), so a fill or line whose look or filter reads a tag is refused, and is split with `features.where` first. A name layer whose look reads tags becomes one layer for each look its names take, in its place, the larger letters over the smaller.
- **Checks**: `Style::new` checks each output an expression writes as a value of its property (a font no screen has is refused, from inside the `step`); a value only known at drawing time (a tag, arithmetic) that the property cannot take leaves it at its default, as `null` does, and a number is rounded where a property counts whole pixels. `keys` is read once, with the names, and is never an expression. The looks a frame resolves to are kept (`Style::resolve`'s cache, by the expressions' values), so a frame costs the evaluation of its expressions: a few hundred small evaluations for a layer of names.
- **Hierarchy**: names are placed by the size of their letters, the largest first, then the top layer first (`place::place`): a city before its quarters, whatever the layer order. A moving screen's remembered places are kept by layer id and text, so a name that changes size keeps its place.

The example styles (`1-bit.js`, `1-bit-night.js`, `terminal.js`, `terminal-day.js`) set their names so: from far the oceans and the capitals, the seas, nearer the cities growing past the towns, their quarters last and smallest (on text, in quieter colours and mixed case), street names only near.

## Painting

`drawland_grid::render(screen, origin, layers)`, `layers` a frame's (`resolve`):

1. Names are placed first (`place.rs`), the layer of the largest letters first, then the top text layer first, each layer's in `labels::named` order: whole pixels or cells, inside the grid, none within `padding` of another, `spacing` between two of one name. A point is centred, or beside its `marker` (right, left, above, below). An area's name goes at its interior point when the box fits inside it. A road's goes level on a straight, level stretch, or one letter under the other on a stretch running down the screen (a bitmap turns the letters a quarter); with neither, level across the road if the road is as long as the name. A moving screen keeps where its names were on the ground (`Recall`, web Mercator metres by layer and text: `Scene::render_recalling`, `Drawer::paint_recalling`; `serve` keeps one a page, by the page's `who`): each layer first places every name where it was (`place::Hints`, in the new picture's grid units; a road's name at the road's nearest point to it, along or level across as before), then the rest as above. It keeps where patterns are read from too (`Recall::origin`): on the ground while the map pans, on the screen while it zooms, since a pattern cannot grow with the map, and read from the world's own origin a zoom step turned the whole sea's `~` over. Without it a road's name hops as the map zooms, since its places along the road are counted from the road's start in screen units; a road's name is on every piece of the road, so the piece it was on is served before the others.
2. Each layer in order: its ink's cells or pixels grown by `halo` are cleared out of what is under it (a bitmap lays the other ink there; text blanks the characters and keeps the background colour), then it is painted:
   - **fill**: `pattern` (8 × 8, `pattern.rs`) or `tone` (ordered Bayer dither); on text the same in Braille dots; `chars` tiled per row; `blocks` (quarter blocks from the dots); `background` (text cells' colour). `opaque` (default) clears the pattern's gaps.
   - **line**: a bitmap line is stepped pixel to pixel (no anti-aliasing), widened by a round brush, dashed by steps. On text: Braille dots (default); box drawing (`box`, `heavy`, `double`, `round`) by cell walks, each cell keeping the sides its line leaves by, so lines that meet join (`┼`), and a stretch slanted more than 20° falls back to Braille rather than a staircase; `ascii` by slope (`- | / \`, `+` where they meet); `char`.
   - **text**: Spleen glyphs on a bitmap, a letter a cell on text; marks.
3. Braille dots, box joins and ASCII strokes in one cell add up across layers of the same colour and style.

Patterns, tones and tiled characters are read at the dot's place in the **world**, not on the screen: `origin(output, screen)` is the screen's top-left in web Mercator dots at the output's scale, so a moved map keeps its waves on the water. Other projections read them from the screen's corner.

## The interface

The interface round a moving screen is drawn in the screen's own medium, not in HTML: character cells on a text screen, Spleen glyphs on 1-bit pixels (LVGL-like), so a terminal and a small screen show it as they show the map. It is laid out on a grid of cells (`ui::Chrome::lay`), from a `Model` the host fills (`chrome::model`), and drawn two ways: over a text screen's cells (`Text::overlay`, a theme's paper and ink and mixes of them), or as pixels (`ui::Pixels`: Spleen 6×12, 5×8 under 200 pixels tall; the page shown and the option chosen inverse, a button underlined in dots, a rule under the bar and over the status line; one bit has no grey, so quieter words are inked like the rest). Cells it leaves empty show the map.

- **Bar**, as draw.land's page has it: `map` `docs` on the left; the style menu (each style a profile file); on a page the font or pixel size menu; on the right the theme menu and `GitHub ↗`. Menus drop down in a box; a narrow bar uses the short labels.
- **Column** on the right (`p`): the place, the scale and zoom, the style and its file with `edit` and `reload`, on a page the size and the window or screen fit, the keys. While it is closed, `+ − ≡` float top right.
- **Status line**: where, how near, the screen's size, the last frame's time; the keys' hint when there is room.
- Each thing pressed is a `Zone` with an action (`open:style`, `style:1-bit`, `theme:ember`, `size:14`, `fit:screen`, `column`, `zoom:1`, `edit`, `reload`, `link:…`; `none` covers what holds no action, so a press there is not the map's). `Ui::act` applies the interface's own; the host does the rest. Keys: `s` style, `t` theme, `f` size, `p` (or `\`) column, `e` edit, Esc closes a menu.
- **Themes**: the page's six (`paper`, `ink`, `silk`, `abyss`, `ember`, `phosphor`) on text, for the interface; on a bitmap `paper` or `ink`, which inverts the map as well.
- **Styles**: the `.js` / `.ts` files beside the profile whose `output` has a `screen` (read by a look at `screen: { kind }`, not run), the profile's own first; `serve` keeps those of its own kind. A style is edited as a file, never in the grid: `drawland view` hands it to `$VISUAL` / `$EDITOR` (stderr goes to `/dev/null` while the map is up, so a log line never scrolls it); a page opens `/edit`, a plain editor in the same theme that saves (⌘S) through `POST /source` and tells the map page by a `BroadcastChannel`.

`≡` is the column's sign, not `☰`: terminals give the trigram two columns, which shifts the rest of the row.

## Hosts

- `drawland map <profile>` writes a screen by the extension of `-o` (`.png`/`.pbm` bitmap, `.txt`/`.ans`/`.html` text, `.png` a picture of text cells; a bitmap written as text is drawn in Braille); `-o -` prints it to the terminal. Unset: `map.png` or `map.ans`.
- `drawland view <profile>` (unix): raw mode on the alternate screen with the mouse reported (SGR), the screen sized to the terminal (a bitmap in Braille, 2 × 4 pixels a cell), the interface over it. Arrows / `hjkl` move a quarter screen and a drag moves with the pointer (`Frame::to_world`), `+`/`-` halve or double the scale and the wheel half that, a click presses what is under it, `r` repaints, `q` (or Esc with no menu open) quits. A press on the interface alone lays it out again over the last map without painting. Every style beside the profile is listed, a bitmap one shown in Braille; switching keeps the place and the ground a cell spans. Events that come while a paint runs are gathered into one move. It changes the graph's output between paints; the script is not run again, so a script that reads `world` sees the first place.
- `drawland serve <profile> [--port 8700]` (any platform): the map in a web page at `http://localhost:<port>/`, one profile a port, so a 1-bit and a text site run side by side as the separate sites they will be; each serves every style of its kind beside the profile, each with its own scene (`?style=` on `/frame`), and a style read again (`/reload`, or saved from the editor) drops its scene. It moves as MapSCII does: the graph is not run per move. `serve.rs` builds a **scene** (`Drawer::scene_graph` → `drawland_grid::Scene`): the `paint` layers evaluated once for a screen three times the view across and down, on the main thread, which owns the `Drawer`. Its vector tiles are as deep as the view's detail (`View::tile_zoom`: a 512-pixel tile's pixel no wider than a dot), as a web map picks them, so a scene is a few dozen tiles at any scale: far out it reads shallow tiles, not the thousand deepest the paper's cap would allow. Each frame (`/frame?lng&lat&scale&w&h`, one thread a kept-alive connection) is that scene's geometry moved and scaled to the view (`Scene::render`: pieces outside the view culled by their boxes, names placed again, patterns read at the view's own world origin), a few milliseconds; a bitmap frame is a PBM, a text frame JSON runs `[text, fg, bg]` a row. A frame moves the map by whole cells (pixels, characters) from the page's last frame at that scale (`Recall`'s phase: where the world's origin sat on the screen), so names, lines and patterns keep their places on it from frame to frame, and says by `X-Shift` how far that leaves it off the view (under half a cell); at a new scale it is drawn exactly where it is, so a zoom stays centred on its point instead of landing half a cell (on text, half a row) off it a different way each step. A view a quarter screen from the scene's edge, or half a zoom step from its scale (`Scene::covers`), asks for a new scene; frames go on from the old one meanwhile (`X-Scene: building`). The page (`serve.html`, built into the binary) has no HTML interface: the map is one canvas, the interface another over it, laid out by the server (`/chrome?style&open&column&theme&size&fit&lat&lng&scale&w&h&ms&loading&error`: on text, rows of runs `[text, fg, bg]` or `[n]` cells of map; on a bitmap, two base64 bit planes, where it covers the map and its ink; with the zones) and fetched after every frame and every press, so the map moves under a still bar. The map canvas moves only as the medium can: by whole characters on text, whole pixels on a bitmap, never scaled. Every move first redraws the last frame shifted by the whole cells the view has gone (when it is the same scale; a frame at another scale, and a globe, `X-Globe`, stay put: a globe turns, it does not slide), on the map's ground colour, then asks for the next frame (one in flight, always for where the map is now). Drag, held arrows / `hjkl`, the wheel and a pinch (zoom about the pointer), `+`/`-` and the tools. A drag holds the Mercator metres a unit, as a web map holds its zoom (the scale true at the centre follows the latitude): held to the scale, every move north or south was a new scale, drawn afresh, and the map shook. Zoom goes by levels, four a doubling (`LEVELS`, a level in about 30 px of wheel): the wheel's turn is gathered until it makes a whole level, and `+`/`-` step through four, so a screen is redrawn at few scales, not at every tick of a trackpad; a press on a zone acts, a press elsewhere on the interface does nothing. Nothing on the page is dragged as an image or selected as text. A bitmap pixel is ×1–×8 **device** pixels (×2 by default on a retina screen); text has a font size. The screen fits the window or is the profile's own `width × height`. While the column is open the map is the part left of it (`Chrome::free`, sent as `free` by `/chrome`; `drawland view` the same), so a globe is round and centred in what is seen of it. On the page a text row is two characters' width tall, as the map takes a cell (a row as tall as the font drew every map a fifth too wide). The `Drawer` keeps the tile source's template (its TileJSON read every ten minutes, not every run) and up to 400 decoded tiles by filter (`TileMemo`): a moving screen reads the same tiles run after run, and reading and decoding them again was most of a globe's time (now 7–60 ms, from 90–230). The style, theme, size, fit and column are kept in the browser. The place and scale are kept in the address (`#lat,lng,scale`). Scenes are web Mercator, north up and unbent (`View::of`); `serve` refuses other outputs.
- Python and Node draw paper only for now (`Drawer::draw` refuses a screen); their faces have `paint`.
- The web page does not paint screens yet. `drawland-grid` does no I/O and builds for WASM as it is; `Text::cell(x, y)` gives a page each cell's character and colours.

## The interface's look

The same cells both ways (`drawland-grid/src/ui.rs`): the bar opens with the site's name on the accent (`Role::Brand`); a menu reads `style terminal ▾`, its key's letter in the theme's accent (`Role::Accent`; underlined, broken at a letter's tail, on 1 bit), its title quieter than its value; the status line is in segments divided by rules, the keys at its right (`s style  t theme …`). The column has a fixed header (`settings`, `×` to close it); under it each section is a card on the column's ground (`Role::Ground`: a pixel in eight on 1 bit, staggered; a shade darker than the cards on text), titled over a rule (`PLACE ────`) with a blank row above and below and two cells of margin at each side, a card on 1 bit framed in a pixel with a two-pixel shadow (`Chrome::cards`, `Pixels::card_edge`). The sections are laid out whole on a sheet (`Chrome::sheet`) and shown from `Model::scroll` rows down, held to what they have (`Chrome::scroll`, sent back by `/chrome`), a scrollbar at the column's edge when they do not fit (a press above or below the thumb turns a page); the wheel over the column scrolls it, on the page and in `drawland view`. Each row of choices is one segmented control (`10px│12px│14px`, `Role::Seam` between): on text a lifted ground, the chosen one inverse; on 1 bit a one-pixel frame with rounded corners, broken where a letter's tail reaches it, the chosen one inverse. Each theme has an accent (`Palette::accent`); the column and menus sit on a ground a shade off the map's. The page takes its settings from its address too (`?column=1&theme=ink&style=…&size=…&rim=over&open=style`), over the browser's.

On a narrow screen (a phone: under `NARROW`, 72 cells) the column is a sheet over the whole screen, its cards' lines whole, the map whole under it (`Chrome::free` stays the screen's width); a bar too narrow for everything keeps its menus, short (`terminal▾`, `ink▾`), and drops the outward links first, then the page's links, then the brand.

## Terra incognita

Zoomed out faster than a wider scene is built, a frame reaches past its scene. There (`drawland-grid/src/incognita.rs`, from `Scene::render_recalling`), a cell and a half inside the scene's edge so that its own shores along that edge go too, the view is the sea, since where there is no land there is sea: the style's `water` layer's look over the whole view (waves when it has none), with rings running out from the screen's middle as a sounding's would (thin rings of brighter characters, or a dot, on text; on 1 bit bands as wide as the gaps between them, `BAND` 12 px, the sea inverted in them), placed by the time the frame is painted. It is a full-view sea painted once and copied where unknown: a few milliseconds a frame at 1600 × 1000. The page asks for frames every tenth of a second while a scene is built, so the rings run until the land is found.

## Far out: a globe

As draw.land's page shows a globe far out, so do `view` and `serve` (`drawland/src/globe.rs`): when the screen's shorter side spans more than `FAR_KM` (4000 km), the graph is drawn in `+proj=ortho` round the view's centre (see [pipeline.md](pipeline.md)) at the view's own size, and past the rim is space (`Picture::globe`), black day or night (a profile that fills the page as land would otherwise fill space too), the rim white on a bitmap and a grey Braille ring on text, names past the rim light on the black (`text::on_space`). Its stars are the real ones (`drawland-grid/src/sky.rs`, `stars.rs`: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue to magnitude 5, 1630 stars, public domain): the eye looks down on the view's centre from afar, north up, so behind the Earth is the sky opposite, the Earth turned under it by the sidereal time at the frame's painting; the screen's shorter side spans `FIELD` (90°) of sky at any zoom. Turning the globe turns the stars the other way, as the far side of anything walked round does. A bright star is a small cross on 1 bit, `*` or `+` on text, a faint one a pixel or a dim `·`; a few hundred projected a frame, about 2 ms with the whole space at 1600 × 1000. A globe is a run of the graph, not a scene moved: `serve` builds one per view on its scene thread (shallow tiles, 50–200 ms), waits up to two seconds for the one asked for, and sends the last one meanwhile (`X-Scene: building`). Dragging turns the globe: the page's pan is web Mercator's, which at the centre is the globe's turn. A drag turns the globe with its size held (the page holds the scale, not web Mercator's metres a unit, which grew toward the poles), to a degree short of a pole; a zoom on a globe is about its centre. Its names are cut at the rim, or with `names past rim` (`n`, `rim=over`) run on past it: `Drawer::paint_globe` paints the names again alone (placing reads only the names, so they land where they did) and `Picture::globe` takes them past the rim, the rim giving way to them. Zoom stops at the whole globe on the screen, a `WHOLE` (0.85) share of its shorter side (`globe::farthest`, `globe::clamp`; the page holds the same bound), whatever the screen's size: a 128 × 64 bitmap goes farther out than an 800 × 480 one.

## Profiles

Day and night: `configs/terminal.js` (text, Venice, the night) and `configs/terminal-day.js` (the same in daylight, on paper); `configs/1-bit.js` (800 × 480 bitmap, Venice, a Nolli plan, the day; main roads white between black edges, a 5-pixel black line under a 3-pixel white one) and `configs/1-bit-night.js` (the day's plan written inverted, `screen.invert`, its buildings dotted so they read as lit windows). Besides: `configs/terminal-ascii.js` (text in plain ASCII, green phosphor), `configs/1-bit-chart.js` (800 × 480, a sea chart: the sea solid, buildings in outline).
